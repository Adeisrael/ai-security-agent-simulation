## What was inspected and how events were selected

All 16 supplied files were read. Routine successful traffic and ordinary operational records were excluded unless they provided context for a flagged event. Records were retained when they showed an explicit alert, blocked or denied attempt, suspicious process or query pattern, unauthorized access, anomalous sensitive-data read, privilege change, or an exposed critical vulnerability. Duplicate alert records and supporting telemetry are grouped with the associated activity. Timestamps are normalized as logged (UTC); no clock correction was inferred.

Correlation used exact shared IP addresses, hostnames, account names, request/alert IDs, and close event sequences. Shared entities are treated as evidence of a relationship, not proof of a common actor. The report distinguishes confirmed control outcomes (for example, blocked/quarantined) from suspected compromise or data exposure.

## Decisions and uncertainties

- **Critical** means evidence of sensitive data successfully read or transferred, suspicious command-and-control egress, or a privileged access change with material exposure. A successful sensitive-object read is treated as potential confirmed exposure; log evidence alone cannot establish who downloaded or retained the object.
- **Medium** means probing, enumeration, exploit attempts, or policy violations where the supplied telemetry shows rejection, blocking, quarantine, or no proven impact.
- **Operational Failure** covers control gaps and vulnerabilities requiring remediation, including the internet-facing production zero-day. Its potential impact is critical, but the evidence supplied reports a finding and overdue patch rather than exploitation.
- WS-044 / 10.20.4.18 appears in endpoint, authentication, and DNS telemetry, but endpoint telemetry names user `alex` while authentication names `j.smith`; same host/IP is a useful lead, not proof of one session or actor. DNS tunneling-like queries occur much later than the file access and cannot be asserted as the same chain.
- The web SQL injection attempts and the edge SSH brute-force use different source IPs and have no supplied linking identifier. They remain separate incidents.
- The external IPs in the logs do not match across the cloud read, proxy C2, Kubernetes probe, and edge SSH attempts. Do not merge them into one campaign based only on similar timing or intent.
- Physical badge activity is dated late on 2026-10-13 UTC, after the daytime events. No network identity or host is correlated to badge E-004182 in the supplied files.
- Data volume, affected-person counts, persistence, credential theft, and whether any blocked attempt succeeded outside the recorded window are not established by these logs.
